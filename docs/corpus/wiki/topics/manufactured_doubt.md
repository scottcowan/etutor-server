# Manufactured Doubt — How Industries Buy Time by Faking Uncertainty

## 100 — Introduction

Sometimes a company doesn't hide the truth outright — it just pays for enough confusing, contradictory-sounding studies that ordinary people can't tell what's real anymore. This buys the company time to keep making money while the argument drags on.

## 200 — Developing

Manufactured doubt works because science is genuinely careful and always leaves a little room for "more research is needed" — real scientists say this all the time about honest open questions. Industries exploit that habit by funding just enough contrarian research to make settled questions (like "does smoking cause cancer?") sound unsettled to a non-expert audience.

## 300 — Intermediate

Key tools of manufactured doubt include industry-funded think tanks that publish "independent" reports, cherry-picking the small number of outlier studies while ignoring scientific consensus, and media strategies that demand "balance" between one scientist representing 97% consensus and one contrarian representing a funded minority view. Asbestos, lead paint, and CFCs/ozone depletion all followed a similar pattern to tobacco before regulation eventually caught up.

## 400 — Advanced / University

Oreskes and Conway's *Merchants of Doubt* (2010) traces a specific network of Cold War-era physicists who moved from defending nuclear weapons research to defending tobacco, acid rain denial, and later climate science denial — reusing the same institutions, funders, and rhetorical techniques across four decades and unrelated industries. The core academic insight is that doubt-manufacturing is not really about persuading experts; its target audience is regulators, judges, and journalists who must decide whether "the science is settled enough" to act.
