# Psychological Operations — How Governments Manipulate Populations

## 100 — Introduction

"Psyops" is short for psychological operations — organised campaigns governments use to change how a group of people thinks or feels, often without the target realising it's happening. Governments have used psyops in wars and at home for a very long time, and much of the history is now declassified and publicly documented.

## 200 — Developing

Psyops can be as simple as dropping leaflets over enemy territory to lower morale, or as sophisticated as radio broadcasts designed to look like independent local news. During the Cold War both the US and USSR ran large psyops programmes aimed at their own citizens as well as each other's, using front organisations, funded media, and cultural programmes to shape opinion without an obvious government fingerprint.

## 300 — Intermediate

The CIA's MKULTRA programme (1953–1973) is the most infamous declassified case: a secret research effort into mind control, interrogation, and behavioural manipulation that included administering drugs like LSD to unwitting subjects, some of whom were seriously harmed. MKULTRA's existence only became public through a 1975 Church Committee congressional investigation and a 1977 Freedom of Information Act request that recovered surviving financial records after most operational documents had been deliberately destroyed.

## 400 — Advanced / University

University-level study of psyops examines the difference between "psywar" (wartime, adversarial) and domestic influence operations, and the legal/ethical debates over each — the 1948 Smith-Mundt Act originally banned the US government from directing propaganda at its own citizens, a restriction loosened by 2013 amendments in ways still debated by scholars of information warfare. Historians treat MKULTRA less as an isolated scandal and more as a case study in how secrecy, weak oversight, and Cold War threat perception together made abuses of psychological research on ordinary citizens possible within a democratic state.
