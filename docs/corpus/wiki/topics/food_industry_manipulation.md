# Big Food — How the Food Industry Shaped What We Eat and Think

## 100 — Introduction

Food companies spend huge amounts of money figuring out exactly what makes people want to keep eating — and then design snacks and drinks around that, sometimes even when it isn't good for us. They also pay for research and advertising that makes unhealthy foods sound healthier than they are.

## 200 — Developing

In the 1960s, the sugar industry funded Harvard researchers to downplay sugar's role in heart disease and shift the blame onto fat instead — a fact only confirmed decades later when internal documents surfaced. Food companies also use engineering tricks like the "bliss point" (the exact combination of salt, sugar, and fat that maximises craving) to make processed foods difficult to stop eating.

## 300 — Intermediate

Front groups funded by food and beverage companies have lobbied against soda taxes, sugar labelling requirements, and school nutrition standards while presenting themselves as neutral nutrition science bodies. Michael Moss's investigative reporting (*Salt Sugar Fat*, 2013) documented internal food-industry memos describing "mouthfeel" and craving engineering as deliberate product design goals, not accidents.

## 400 — Advanced / University

Nutrition science itself is genuinely difficult (long-term diet studies are expensive and hard to control for confounds), which is exactly the vulnerability industry-funded research exploits — much like tobacco exploited genuine scientific caution about causation. University-level analysis of food industry manipulation compares it directly to the tobacco and pharmaceutical playbooks: fund friendly studies, capture regulatory bodies through revolving-door hiring, and target marketing at children and lower-income communities who have fewer resources to resist persuasive advertising.
