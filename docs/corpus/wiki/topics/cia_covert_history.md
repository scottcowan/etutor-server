# Covert Operations — What Governments Do in Secret (and How We Know)

## 100 — Introduction

Sometimes a government secretly tries to change what happens in another country — helping remove a leader, for example — without admitting it publicly. We often only find out decades later, when official documents get released.

## 200 — Developing

The CIA carried out or helped carry out several secret operations to overthrow foreign governments during the Cold War, including in Iran (1953), Guatemala (1954), and Chile (1973). These operations combined propaganda, funding opposition groups, economic pressure, and sometimes direct support for coups — and were officially denied for years afterward.

## 300 — Intermediate

Declassification is how we know: the 1975 Church Committee investigation, decades of Freedom of Information Act requests, and the CIA's own 2013 acknowledgment of its role in the 1953 Iranian coup (Operation Ajax) turned "conspiracy theory" into documented history. In Chile, declassified cables confirmed the US provided support to opponents of elected president Salvador Allende ahead of the 1973 coup that installed General Pinochet, whose government then committed well-documented human rights abuses.

## 400 — Advanced / University

University-level analysis of covert operations distinguishes formal covert action (authorised secret operations with legal "findings" under US law since 1974) from unauthorised or improperly authorised actions later investigated as scandals, such as Iran-Contra. Scholars studying these cases weigh a genuine tension: covert action is a real, sometimes legally sanctioned tool of foreign policy in a dangerous world, but its secrecy makes democratic accountability structurally difficult — the public and even many elected officials often cannot evaluate a covert programme's costs and consequences until long after the decisions were made.
